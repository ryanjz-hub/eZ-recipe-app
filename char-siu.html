Char Siu 叉燒

Origin


Southern China Guangdong

Originating from Guangdong China, a very popular dish among the Cantonese regions of the province. It is unclear how long the process to create char siu has been around but it is estimated to be upwards of 3,000 years. With the first signs of char siu appearing in the Zhou Dynasty. Additionally traces of the traditional process of roasting the pork can be seen across East Asia. With one prominent figure being Japan's chashu pork. A ramen topping which originates from the Chinese char siu. Char siu is often eaten with carbohydrates like rice or stuffed in buns due to its rich protein properties. Allowing for a more balanced meal. The recipe below should provide a fairly authentic version of the char siu.

About this recipe

  • Estimated prep time: 10-15 minutes (Does not include overnight waiting time).
  • Estimated cooking time: 50 minutes.
  • Total time: ~1 hour.

Ingredients

  • 3 lbs Boneless pork shoulder/pork butt.
  • 1⁄4 Cup granulated white sugar.
  • 2 Tsp Salt.
  • 1⁄4 Tsp White pepper.
  • 1⁄2 Tsp Spice blend of your choice.
  • 1⁄2 Tsp Sesame oil.
  • 1 Tbsp Rice wine or cooking wine.
  • 1 Tbsp Soy sauce.
  • 1 Tbsp Hoisin sauce.
  • 2 Tsp Molasses.

Prep

  1. Slice pork into 2-3inch strips/chunks. For this recipe, leaving excess fat is fine as it will render off when cooking and add flavor.
  2. Combine in a large bowl with sugar, salt, spice blend, white pepper, sesame oil, hoisin sauce, soy sauce, cooking wine, molasses, and garlic. This will be your marinade.
  3. Evenly spread marinade on pork pieces, leave behind roughly 2 Tbps of marinade mixture behind. We will be saving this for later.
  4. Place pork pieces in a large bowl or baking tray to rest. Cover and leave in refrigerator for 8-10hrs.

Recipe

  1. Set up two racks in oven, one positioned on the upper third part of the oven and another one directly a rack below.
  2. Preheat oven to 475°F or 246°C.
  3. Apply aluminum foil on a large baking pan and then place a metal rack above. The metal rack will help prevent the pork pieces from sticking to the pan.
  4. Place pork pieces flat on metal rack.
  5. Transfer tray onto the top rack that we set up earlier.
  6. Prepare a water bath inside of a oven safe tray with roughly 11⁄2 Cups of water on the rack below.

    Image reference
  7. Roast char siu for 25-30minutes.
    • Check char siu every 10 minutes. It may be useful to have a thermometer inside oven to keep track of temperature as every oven is different.
    • After 10minutes reduce the oven temperature to 375°F or 190°C.
  8. After 25 minutes, flip the char siu over. If there is no water left in the pan below, refill it.
  9. Continue baking the char siu for another 15minutes.
    • Remember to check the char siu every 10minutes and reduce temperature if it is too hot. i.e. signs of burning.
  10. After about 40-45 minutes of roasting, baste the pork with the marinade we saved earlier on both sides. Then roast for 10 minutes. Before removing it from the oven to cool.

Additional information

  • You may want to check your char siu's internal temperature with a thermometer if you are unsure if its fully cooked. A temperature of 160° F or 71° C would be the most optimal temperature.
